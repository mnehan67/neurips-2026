Transformer MLP Transplants Depend on the Receiving State

Muhammed Nehan

Ajman University

Accepted at MusIML 2026 (Poster)
Accepted at NewInML 2026 (Poster)
OpenReview Code Reproducibility

NeurIPS 2026 workshop paper

Hold the same natural MLP displacement fixed, change only where it is written, and the downstream effect changes in both size and direction.

The question

When an internal update is moved between forward passes, is its effect a property of the update alone, or of the update together with the state that receives it?

1

Compute one natural update

For host i and donor j, form the natural MLP displacement Δu = uj − ui.

2

Freeze it

Apply that exact vector, with identical norm and direction, to many natural receiver states.

3

Measure receiver dependence

Measure exact next-token KL and trace how the same causal footprint changes through the downstream network.

Main finding: an internal update does not carry one context-free effect. The receiving state is part of the intervention.

Abstract

Activation patching and steering move internal updates between forward passes and often treat a moved update as if it carried a portable effect. Ordinary transplants cannot test this, because the update and the state that receives it change together. We isolate the receiver with a crossed-receiver design: a natural transformer MLP displacement Δu = uj − ui is computed once, frozen, and physically added to many natural receiver states of Qwen3-8B and Mistral-7B; its exact next-token distributional effect is measured as a KL divergence.

The same displacement is mild in some receivers and disruptive in others; across 31 receivers its effect typically spans more than three orders of magnitude. An established receiver-local Fisher score ranks these full-dose effects consistently, with median within-displacement Spearman correlation 0.858–0.928 across all six model–bucket replications; the corresponding unweighted response reaches at most 0.568. The association remains after removing generic displacement strength and receiver fragility, with residual correlation 0.50–0.68.

The receiver also changes the direction of the effect: footprints of one displacement diverge across receivers, yet receiver-specific low-rank maps fitted on other displacements predict held-out footprints better than a global map. The effect of an internal update depends on where it is written, in both size and direction.

Method

The crossed-receiver design separates the update from its destination.

Crossed-receiver intervention

For a fixed displacement Δud, we run many receivers rk natively and with rk ← rk + Δud. The primary statistic ranks receivers while the physical intervention is held fixed.

The exact endpoint is the forward KL between the native and patched next-token distributions.

Receiver-local diagnostic

We use the standard Fisher pullback through the downstream suffix as a receiver-local sensitivity diagnostic. The geometry itself is not claimed as new; the empirical question is whether it ranks the full-dose effect of the same natural update across different receivers.

We also compare attribution, norm, alignment, matched-magnitude, and physical equal-norm controls.

Results

Receiver dependence is large, ordered, pair-specific, and visible in downstream causal direction.

1. The same displacement has very different effects across receivers

Across 31 alternative receivers, the largest and smallest exact effects of one displacement differ by a median factor of roughly 104.4–104.8 in Qwen3-8B and 103.3–103.6 in Mistral-7B. Receiver-local Fisher ranking reaches 0.871 / 0.872 / 0.858 in Qwen and 0.911 / 0.928 / 0.928 in Mistral across B1/B2/B3.

A physical equal-norm transplant control preserves receiver ordering exactly at the median (ρ = 1.000 in both models), showing that displacement scale does not create the ordering.

3. Pair-specific structure remains after generic receiver fragility

Receiver identity explains much of the rank variance, but 18–25% remains as non-additive residual variation. After removing additive displacement and receiver effects, Fisher residual correlation remains 0.504–0.520 in Qwen3-8B and 0.658–0.675 in Mistral-7B, above the matched unweighted response in all six crossed replications.

4. The receiver changes causal direction, not only magnitude

Directional fidelity between the original host footprint and the receiver footprint falls to 0.030 at Qwen3-8B's final layer and 0.037 at Mistral-7B's. Yet receiver-specific rank-16 maps generalize to unseen displacements: at the final layer, held-out cosine reaches 0.316 in Qwen and 0.241 in Mistral, versus 0.086 / 0.109 for one global map and −0.023 / 0.028 for wrong-receiver maps.

The result is deliberately modest: the receiver-dependent transformation contains a reproducible receiver-specific component, while substantial update-specific variation remains.

5. Numerical and longer-horizon checks

Exact FP32 suffix-JVP audit

On 16 control-panel directions per model, FP32 centered differences and exact FP32 suffix JVPs have ρ = 1.000 and median relative Fisher-score error of about 0.21% (Qwen) and 0.20% (Mistral).

Natural states come from the FP16 pipeline and only the downstream suffix is recomputed in FP32, so this is not a full-model FP32 JVP. FP16 scores for the same 16 cases agree less closely with the exact FP32 scores (ρ = 0.785 / 0.544), consistent with finite-precision effects; the large-scale Fisher score is therefore treated as a ranking diagnostic. The audit varies displacement direction at one fixed receiver and is not a replication of the primary receiver-ranking statistic.

Teacher-forced continuation

Correlation with cumulative KL remains positive through 32 future teacher-forced positions: 0.857 → 0.565 for Qwen and 0.930 → 0.812 for Mistral from h = 1 to 32.

Teacher forcing does not establish persistence under unconstrained generation.

Independent pretraining

Across six independently pretrained Pythia-70M runs under ordinary transplants, all controlled partial correlations are positive; median ρ = 0.800, 95% bootstrap interval [0.742, 0.827].

This is an ordinary-transplant replication, not a crossed-receiver replication.

Scope

What the result does and does not establish.

The crossed design identifies receiver dependence because the intervention vector is physically fixed within each displacement. It does not establish that an MLP update is a context-independent semantic function, and the low-rank receiver maps are not complete or deterministic receiver operators.

The study is restricted to final-token MLP interventions and primarily next-token behavior. Teacher-forced continuation is supportive but is not free generation. The paper is diagnostic and proposes no steering or editing method; preserving an intervention's intended causal effect across receivers is a natural direction for future work.
